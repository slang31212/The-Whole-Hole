## 0. The one thing to internalize before anything else

**We are not selling a data center, a wind platform, or an oil rig. We are selling a standardized, class-approved, low-motion floating deck — leased by the day — whose unrivaled advantage is capital cost.**

An MPSS hull costs **~$25–45M all-in** (serial target $25–35M). The large semisubmersible and drillship hulls it competes against cost **$425M–$1.2B** — **10× to 40× more**. Because hull cost is the denominator of every return calculation, this is a *structural* advantage that does not depend on how we set assumptions:

> **MPSS can charge a day-rate far below the market and still earn a dramatically higher yield on hull capital than any comparable vessel.** The edge is in the CAPEX, not the day-rate. That single fact is the spine of every pitch, every proposal, and every fundraising conversation.

Everything below is built to convert that advantage into signed, financeable, long-term leases.

## 1. Executive summary

**The business (MILC):** own standardized MPSS hulls and dry-lease them to operators on **10–15 year firm terms at a target day-rate of $200,000–$500,000/day**, delivered **30–36 months from signature**. MILC's obligations are narrow — hull integrity, class maintenance, insurance structure — *not* operations. That narrow-obligation, contracted-cash-flow structure is exactly what offshore lenders already underwrite (the FPSO financing template).

**Why it works, in one breath:** a ~$45M hull earning $200–500k/day pays back in **3–8 months** (pre-financing) and throws off **$68–173M/yr in unit EBITDA**. A 50–70-year hull gets re-let 3–5 times over its life. Five units at the midpoint rate = **~$602.6M/yr fleet EBITDA**.

**Marketing's three jobs:**
1. **Make the capital-efficiency case undeniable** — arm every buyer and lender with the CAPEX-advantage math and the independent validation behind it.
2. **Land the anchor lessee** — the single signature that lets us finance and cut steel. No speculative construction; the lease comes first.
3. **Aggregate demand across markets** — MPSS is one standardized hull serving grid/HVDC, floating wind, hydrogen, data/compute, and (Phase 3) production. Marketing is the demand aggregator that makes a serial fabrication run bankable.

**The 12-month prize:** a **signed anchor-lease LOI** from a Priority-1 segment (a grid operator or diversified-energy major), a **confirmed serial fabrication cost** from Hanwha Ocean, and a **current class engagement** — the three gating items that turn the plan from concept to financeable project.

## 2. The asset & its credibility (our biggest sales weapon)

The MPSS is a four-column, ring-pontoon semi-submersible built from **standardized flat-panel box-girder blocks** — no delicate bracing — using high-automation welding at **~18 man-hours/tonne vs. 150–180 for conventional semis**. Reference specs (1988 Seaways data; Lloyd's pre-certification file 00/OA/89001; Glasgow University tests):

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Main deck area | 8,100 m² |
| Deck payload | 10,000 t (design 12,500 t; up to 30,000 t studied) |
| Displacement | ~53,000 t |
| Operating draft | 27.5 m constant; deballasted to ~6 m for port access |
| Motion | Heave period ~24 s, pitch/roll ~42 s; heave ±2.5 m in 15 m seas |
| Hull fatigue life | 50–70 years (Faulkner) — distinct from 25–30 yr SCR life |
| Storage variant | SWIS (Semi With Integral Storage): up to 1,750,000 bbl in-hull |

**The 35-year independent-validation spine — this kills the "can it really work?" objection, which is the #1 barrier to a novel offshore lease:**
- **Lloyd's Register — Department of Safety Technology** risk analysis (Dr David S. Aldwinckle, Dept. Head): *"there are no inherent weaknesses in the MPSS design concepts, systems and arrangements."* Lloyd's *"fully accept all of the design principles"* and confirmed it *"can be approved for the intended service"*; **main scantlings approved**.
- **University of Glasgow** — 9-month model tank-test programme (Professors **Faulkner & Incecik**, marine structural dynamics).
- **W.S. Atkins Oil & Gas Ltd.** — independent structural/hydrodynamic analysis. **ABS** and **DNV** pre-qualification/review on record.
- **1995 IMT / Enterprise Oil study** — independent *commercial* validation; 12 international shipyards priced it; full cost + schedule; *"technically feasible and commercially viable."*

**Finance and leasing were always native to this design.** Seaways' own historical services included *"Finance and MPSS leasing"*; in **1989 Lloyds Bank Canada** (Tim Plumtre) was ready to finance MPSS builds in Brazil and Newfoundland. We are re-activating a proven, bankable model — not inventing one.

**Disclosure control (protect the IP that is the moat):** General Arrangement behind NDA only; full technical package only under a formal engineering partnership or licence; **GVA-lineage yards are the highest disclosure risk**; lease documentation transfers **no design rights**.

- **B. Root cause is **platform motion**. MPSS's low-motion geometry (heave ~24 s, pitch/roll ~42 s — out of the 8–16 s swell-resonance band) cuts cable fatigue an estimated **40–50%**, worth **€2.8–5.8M per vessel over 25 years** and a **fleet NPV of €420–900M by Year 25**. *"Operators buy cable performance, not complexity."* Recommended proof path: a 1–2 vessel instrumented pilot in the Celtic or North Sea.

- **Asset finance / CFO:** dry-lease = clean, financeable cash flow; anchor-lease-financed, off their balance sheet; payback and yield math.
- **Insurer / risk / lender:** cable-fatigue reduction, class approval, hull fatigue life, low-motion data — lower loss ratios and premiums.

## 5. The offer & commercial model

**Phase 1 — Bare-hull dry lease.** MILC owns the hull; lessee fits and operates topsides. Obligations limited to hull integrity, class, insurance structure. **$200–500k/day, 10–15 yr firm.** This is the whole near-term business.

**Phase 2 — Mooring package (Years 3–6).** Hull + installed station-keeping leased as one asset. Priced as an adder at the same lessor yield on mooring capex (~$10–20M site-dependent → roughly **$4,000–8,000/day adder**; firm only after site-specific design). Converts MILC from hull lessor to station-keeping infrastructure provider. Entry route: partner with an established mooring contractor for the first two packages, internalize from unit three.

**Phase 3 — SCR-integrated production host (Years 5+).** The coupled low-motion-host + Steel Catenary Riser architecture — the **LANG System's original invention scope**. Rates built line-item against field economics; reference category is FPSO leasing ($540–780k/day on $0.7–1.3B capex). Begins once the leasing base generates recurring cash.

**Commercial principles:**
- **Anchor lease first, then finance, then steel.** No speculative construction. The signed lease is the key that unlocks everything.
- **Sell yield, not sticker.** Use the capital-efficiency math: we can sit *below* market day-rates and still win on return. A first-of-class discount is acceptable on unit one **if term ≥ 10 yr**; recover on units 2–5.
- **Two rate cards:** an internal cost basis (never external) and a **two-page lessee-facing rate card in market-value framing only**.
- **Redeployability & residual are part of the pitch** — a class-maintained hull with 35+ years of remaining fatigue life at first-term end is real terminal value, not scrap.

## 12. Raising funds — capitalizing the MPSS fleet

**The core structure (the FPSO template, and MILC's whole financing thesis): the anchor lease is signed first, and the hull is financed against the lease — the lender underwrites the charter, not the concept.** A dry-lease income stream with narrow lessor obligations is precisely what offshore lenders already know how to underwrite. Marketing/BD's job is to manufacture that anchor lease; the capital then follows the contract.

### 12.1 What we're funding — and how small the first ask really is
- **First unit: ~$45M hull + ~$5M development/owner's costs ≈ $50M.** That is the entire capital need to put the proof unit on hire — trivially small against the $68–173M/yr of EBITDA one unit produces and the $425M–$1.2B cost of every competing hull.
- **Development capital (now, pre-lease):** the ~$5M for FEED, class re-engagement, fabrication confirmation, and the traction campaign. Riskiest tranche → founder/strategic equity or grant.
- **Hull capex (post-anchor-lease):** financed against the signed lease → offshore project / asset finance debt (the cheap workhorse).
- **Fleet growth:** each of the 50–70-year hulls, re-let 3–5×, deleverages after ~20-yr amortization — the second and third lease terms run on a substantially unlevered asset. Strong recurring collateral for scaling the fleet toward the long-range ramp.

### 12.2 The capital stack (cheapest → most expensive)
- **Offshore asset / project finance debt** — lent against the anchor lease's cash flows; the FPSO model; **priority once the LOI/lease exists.**
- **Export credit agency (ECA) support** — the build ties to a major yard (**Hanwha Ocean, Korea**); ECA guarantees materially lower financing cost. *Structural advantage — the in-country/serial-build story reinforces it.*
- **Anchor-lessee / strategic co-investment** — a grid operator or energy major co-funds "their" hull for priority access.
- **Infrastructure & energy-transition equity** — appetite for contracted, asset-backed, ESG-aligned cash flows; the cable-risk and grid-enabling angles fit cleanly.
- **Green / sustainability-linked finance** — floating-wind enablement, hydrogen, low-motion cable-integrity, and the Crystal Sea water/clean-power variants genuinely qualify → cheaper tranches.
- **Development finance / multilaterals (DFIs)** — for sovereign or emerging-market siting and the in-country-build local-content story.
- **Grants / non-dilutive** — realistic for the **cable-risk pilot** (a fundable, instrumented R&D proof) and the **Crystal Sea** environmental variant. *This workspace has live funder/grant-discovery tools (Granted, Kindora) connected — say the word and I'll pull a targeted list of ocean-health / clean-energy / offshore-wind-innovation funders and open grants for the pilot and the Crystal Sea variant.*

**Recommended path:** small founder/strategic development tranche now → **anchor lease** → project-finance debt + ECA for the hull (majority of the $45M) → strategic/infra-equity for the balance and the next units; green tranches and grant/DFI money where the wind/hydrogen/water/cable-pilot profiles qualify. **Precedent on our side:** Seaways has done this before — Lloyds Bank Canada was ready to finance MPSS builds in 1989.

1. **A quantified, urgent problem** — the €61.5B cable-failure crisis and the ~$1.1B/GW substation gap that developers must fund anyway.
2. **A proven, not speculative, asset** — Lloyd's/ABS/DNV/W.S. Atkins/Glasgow; 35 years of validation. *De-risk the "novel" objection immediately.*
3. **An unbeatable capital structure** — 10–40× lower hull cost; underprice the market and still out-yield every comparable.
4. **A financeable model** — dry lease, narrow obligations, anchor-lease-first, FPSO template.
5. **Anchor demand** — the signed/near-signed Priority-1 lease (the linchpin).
6. **Unit economics** — ~$45M hull, 3–8 month payback, $68–173M/yr unit EBITDA, ~$602.6M/yr at five units.
7. **The flywheel** — lease → hull finance → build → cash flow → re-let a 50–70-yr asset 3–5× → finance the next unit.
8. **The ask & use of funds** — the ~$5M development tranche now, and the hull-finance package on anchor-lease signature.
