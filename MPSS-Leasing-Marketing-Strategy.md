## 1. Executive summary

**The play:** Turn MPSS from a product you sell into a *revenue stream you own*. Leasing converts a big, scary upfront customer price into a small recurring payment — which lowers the buying barrier, locks in retention, and (critically) creates a predictable, financeable cash-flow asset that we can borrow and raise against.

**The three jobs of marketing here:**
1. **Manufacture demand** — make the right buyers understand that leasing MPSS is cheaper, faster, and lower-risk than the alternative (owning, doing nothing, or a competitor).
2. **Compress the sales cycle** — leasing is a financial decision as much as a product decision, so our content and sales motion must de-risk it (proof, guarantees, transparent math).
3. **Fuel the capital story** — every signed lease is both revenue *and* collateral. Marketing's traction metrics are the raw material of the fundraise (Section 12).

**Where we'll win:** on **total cost of access, uptime/reliability, and speed-to-value** — not on lowest sticker price. We are the "never worry about it again" option.

## 5. Pricing & the leasing offer

Marketing and pricing are inseparable here — the *offer structure* is a marketing tool.

**Lease structures to offer (menu, not all at once):**
- **Standard term lease** — 12/24/36 months, fixed monthly. Predictable; our default.
- **Flex / short-term** — higher rate, cancelable; captures the risk-averse and the seasonal.
- **Per-deployment / usage-based** — pay per use; lowers the entry barrier for skeptics.
- **Lease-to-own** — for buyers who psychologically need an ownership endpoint.
- **Bundled service tiers** — Good/Better/Best (basic access → premium SLA + priority support).

**Pricing principles:**
- Anchor on **value/TCO**, never on cost-plus. Lead with "vs. buying it costs $X, you save $Y."
- Make the **entry offer frictionless** — a low-commitment pilot or first-term discount to beat inertia.
- Build in **expansion hooks** — additional units, upgraded SLA, add-on services (this drives NRR > 100%).
- Keep terms **radically transparent** — hidden fees kill trust and lengthen sales cycles.

**Offer to test first:** a **risk-reversal pilot** — "Lease one MPSS for `[30–90] days`; if it doesn't hit `[the outcome]`, walk away." Nothing accelerates a leasing decision like removing the downside.

## 12. Raising funds — capitalizing the leasing business

**The core insight:** a leasing business has a *marketing/sales engine* AND a *capital engine*, and they feed each other. Every signed lease is a **contracted, recurring cash flow** — which is exactly what lenders and investors like to fund. Marketing's job is to produce the traction that de-risks the capital raise; the capital's job is to buy the assets that let us sign more leases. **Raise against the book, not just the idea.**

### 12.1 What we're actually funding
Two distinct capital needs — fund them with different instruments:
1. **Asset capital** — money to acquire/build the MPSS units we lease out. This should be financed *cheaply* with debt, because it's collateralized by hard assets and predictable lease payments. Don't burn precious equity on hardware.
2. **Growth capital** — money for team, marketing, and runway. This is riskier, so it's funded by equity or, if we're mission-driven, grants.

### 12.2 The capital stack — sources in rough order of cost
- **Asset-backed / equipment financing & leasing lines** — a lender funds the units against the assets + lease contracts. Cheapest way to scale the fleet. *Priority #1 once we have signed leases to show.*
- **Revenue-based / receivables financing** — borrow against recurring lease payments. Non-dilutive, scales with the book.
- **Venture debt** — complements an equity round; extends runway without heavy dilution.
- **Equity (angel / seed / VC / strategic)** — for the growth engine and to signal ambition. Most expensive capital; use it for the things debt won't fund.
- **Grants / non-dilutive (if mission-aligned)** — foundations and public programs, *if* MPSS serves a public-good use (climate, health, access, resilience, etc.). Free money, but slow and restricted. **Note:** this workspace has grant/funder-discovery tools (Granted, Kindora) connected — if MPSS has any nonprofit or public-benefit angle, I can pull a live, targeted list of matching funders and open grants. Just confirm the mission angle.
- **Strategic / customer prepay** — a large customer or supplier co-funds the fleet in exchange for priority access or better terms.

**Recommended blend to start:** asset-backed debt for the units + a modest equity/grant round for the growth engine. This keeps dilution low and ties financing cost to a self-liquidating asset.

### 12.3 The investor/lender narrative (marketing owns this)
Same storytelling discipline as customer marketing, aimed at capital providers. The story arc:
1. **Big, real problem** — quantified cost of the status quo MPSS solves.
2. **Why leasing wins** — lower barrier, stickier revenue, recurring cash flow, financeable assets.
3. **Traction** — signed leases, pipeline, retention, unit economics (this is why Sections 1–10 come first).
4. **Unit economics that work** — LTV:CAC ≥ 3:1, CAC payback, lease margin, asset payback period, default rate.
5. **Use of funds** — exactly what the money buys and the ARR it produces.
6. **The flywheel** — capital → more units → more leases → more recurring revenue → more borrowing capacity → repeat.

### 12.4 Fundraising materials to build (reuse the marketing assets)
- **Pitch deck** (10–12 slides): problem, solution, market, business/leasing model, traction, unit economics, competition, team, use of funds, the ask.
- **One-page teaser** for cold outreach.
- **Financial model**: fleet build, lease revenue, utilization, default assumptions, cohort/unit economics, cash-flow and payback.
- **Data room**: lease contracts (anonymized), customer references, cap table, financials, legal.
- **The "asset + cash-flow" schedule** — the single doc that makes lenders comfortable: what we own, what it earns, over what term.

### 12.5 The fundraising funnel (run it like a sales pipeline)
1. **Target list** — right lenders/investors for a leasing/asset business (equipment-finance lenders, revenue-based financiers, funds that do capital-intensive/asset-heavy or your vertical; relevant grant-makers if mission-aligned).
2. **Warm intros > cold** — map the network first.
3. **Teaser → deck → meeting → data room → term sheet → close.**
4. **Run it in parallel, create urgency, drive to competing term sheets.**
5. **Coverage math:** to close `[N]` checks, put ~`10–15×` targets at the top of the funnel.

### 12.6 Sequencing (tie the raise to traction milestones)
- **Now → Q2:** bootstrap/seed the first few units; produce reference leases and unit-economics proof. *You raise more, cheaper, after you can show a working lease book.*
- **Q2:** open the asset-backed debt line on the strength of signed leases.
- **Q3:** run the equity/grant raise on Q1–Q2 traction (the plan's headline fundraising window).
- **Ongoing:** scale the debt facility as the lease book grows — the recurring, non-dilutive workhorse.

**Milestones that unlock better terms:** first `[N]` signed leases · `$[FILL]` ARR · sub-`[X]%` default rate · CAC payback under `[12–18]` months · a repeatable, provable sales motion. **Every one of these is a marketing deliverable — which is the whole point: the marketing plan *is* the fundraising plan.**
