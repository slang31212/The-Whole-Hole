Founding Investor Memorandum

The Opportunity

Offshore industries still commission the floating asset beneath every project one at a time — bespoke, engineered from a blank sheet, slow to build and difficult to reuse. Each host is a sunk, single-purpose cost rather than a durable, transferable asset.

Other industries retired that problem long ago by standardising the asset underneath the activity: the airframe beneath the airline, the container beneath global trade, the building beneath the tenant. Ownership of the standardised asset — not the activity on top of it — became the enduring, compounding business.

Seaways proposes the same shift offshore. The Multi-Purpose Semi-Submersible (MPSS) is a standardised, low-motion offshore host designed to be owned, leased, redeployed and reused across many industries over a service life measured in decades. This memorandum concerns that ownership model — not a single hull, and not a single project.

The Investment Thesis

The proposed business is not the sale of a hull, nor participation in one development. It is the formation of a fleet-owning offshore-infrastructure company, built on a single discipline:

Build once · Lease repeatedly · Redeploy globally · Retain the asset · Compound fleet value.

The nearest reference models are conceptual, not exact: aircraft leasing — own the airframe, lease it to the operator, redeploy it on expiry; long-life core infrastructure — durable, contracted, inflation-resilient cash flows; and prime commercial real estate — a hard asset that outlives its tenants. The MPSS extends that logic to the one offshore asset that has never been standardised: the host itself.

Why MPSS Matters

High-value offshore developments depend on dynamic connections to the seabed and the grid — steel catenary risers, CO₂ injection risers, dynamic power and export cables, umbilicals and control systems. Host-platform motion drives cyclic loading, fatigue, maintenance and downtime in exactly these connections — the components most expensive to repair and most costly to lose.

The MPSS was engineered as a low-motion host to protect those connections and underwrite long, uninterrupted service life. The supporting hydrodynamic evidence is available for due diligence.

Why the day-rate is a discount, not a premium. Production hosts of comparable capacity — bp's Argos (~US$9 billion Mad Dog 2), BP Thunder Horse, Woodside's Scarborough (~US$12.5 billion) — are multi-billion-dollar, multi-year bespoke builds. The MPSS delivers that class of host from a standardised ~US$45 million hull and pulls first production forward by years. The lease rate reflects the value delivered — a decade of risk removed — not the cost to build. That spread is the asset.

Founding Partner Position

Seaways is inviting a small number of long-term founding capital partners — not conducting a broad raise — to help complete commercial readiness, finance Hull 1, establish the first reference asset, support customer reservation activity for subsequent hulls, and build the foundation of a fleet-leasing company. The business is early by design: the intent is to shape it alongside its founding owners, not to place a finished product.

The objective is not to build one offshore platform. It is to establish ownership of standardised offshore infrastructure that multiple industries can lease for decades.
