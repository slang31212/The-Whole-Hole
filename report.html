01 · The Thesis

Own the standardised asset underneath the activity.

Every offshore industry still commissions the floating host beneath its project one at a time — bespoke, engineered from a blank sheet, slow to build, and difficult to reuse. It is a sunk, single-purpose cost. Other industries retired that problem long ago by standardising the asset underneath the activity — the airframe beneath the airline, the container beneath global trade, the building beneath the tenant — and owning that standardised asset became the durable, compounding business.

Seaways proposes the same shift offshore. The MPSS is designed to be owned, leased, redeployed and reused across many industries over a service life measured in decades. This is not the sale of one hull, and not one project — it is the formation of a fleet-owning offshore-infrastructure company.

02 · Why a Family Office Leans In

Everything a patient balance sheet is built to own.

Hard-asset collateral

Capital sits behind classed steel — a real, redeployable host, not a covenant or goodwill. If a charter ends, the asset is still there, still classed, still leaseable to the next field.

Contracted, uncorrelated income

Revenue is a multi-year lease on a physical asset — driven by offshore field economics and steel availability, not by public-equity multiples or rate cycles. A genuine diversifier.

Downside built in by design

Tested to a 37.5 m survival wave with broken-mooring survival. The engineering that keeps it producing in a storm is the same engineering that protects the residual value your capital is secured against.

A second and third life

When one field winds down, the same hull floats off and re-leases — added tiebacks, power, CCS, hydrogen, data. Optionality that keeps the asset earning far beyond a single charter.

03 · The Moat

A standardised hull that does a billion-dollar host's job.

Offshore day-rates are set by the value of the work, not by what the host cost to build. Production hosts of comparable capacity are multi-billion-dollar, multi-year bespoke builds. The MPSS delivers that class of host from a standardised, commodity-steel hull — and pulls first oil forward by years. That spread is the asset.

04 · Independent Validation

Not our word — Aberdeen University's.

Aberdeen University Petroleum & Economic Consultants (AUPEC), directed by Prof. Alexander G. Kemp — specialist adviser to the UK House of Commons Select Committee on Energy — independently modelled the MPSS across the North Sea and found it “anticipated to cost a fraction of a conventional purpose-built semi-submersible.” Across five model fields it raised post-tax returns to investors in every case — turning marginal fields into attractive investments.

05 · The Asset

07 · The Business Model

Build once. Compound the fleet.

The economics are a leasing business, not a project. Value compounds along one track:

  1. Build once
  2. Lease repeatedly
  3. Redeploy globally
  4. Retain the asset
  5. Compound fleet value

Each hull earns across multiple charters and fields; the fleet — not any single lease — is the asset.

Recurring lease income

Multi-year bare-boat charters throw off contracted cash from the first day of hire.

Redeployment upside

Float-off / float-on to the next field extends earning life and supports residual value.

Standardisation leverage

Repeatable, panel-line builds mean known cost, known motions, known schedule — hull after hull.

08 · Investor Economics & Structures
