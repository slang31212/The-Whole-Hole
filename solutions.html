The Platform Product

The floating host is the hard part. So we made it standard.

Whether you are moving gigawatts of offshore-wind power to shore, firming that power with storage, or producing hydrocarbons in deep water, the challenge is the same: a large, stable, classed floating platform delivered on a predictable schedule. The MPSS solves it once. Three topside packages, one validated 15,000-tonne hull — approved by Lloyd's Register, ABS and DNV.

Standard hull, any mission

Ring pontoon, four box columns, buoyant box deck. The identical structure carries a converter, a battery hub, or a production train. The mission lives in the topside.

Low-motion by design

A deep-draft ring pontoon sees roughly 40% of surface wave height — effectively motionless in sub-12 m seas. Good for converters, batteries and risers alike.

Leaseable, not bespoke

The host becomes pre-positioned inventory you can lease, bareboat, JV or buy — and redeploy to a new mission when the field, farm or grid need changes.

MPSS-HVDC Floating Converter Station · RHODÉ-class

A floating HVDC converter station — classed, standardized, and ready before the wind farm is.

Offshore-wind converter platforms are today's critical-path item: bespoke jackets and topsides, multi-year queues, and schedules that slip the whole farm. The MPSS-HVDC puts a full MMC converter hall, transformers and GIS switchgear on the standard MPSS hull — a repeatable product instead of a one-off megaproject. Sea-water-cooled, fiber-linked to shore, and dynamically cabled to the array.

Why it wins

  • Off the critical path. A validated hull delivered in ~14 months de-risks the single item most likely to delay a wind farm.
  • Low-motion platform protects converter equipment and eases dynamic-cable fatigue at the J-tubes.
  • Standard = bankable. Known hull, known motions, known class basis (ABS/DNV) shortens the approvals cycle.
  • Redeployable. Float-off / float-on lets the station move to the next zone when a project completes.
Built for

Transmission system operators, offshore-wind developers, and HVDC OEMs / EPCs looking to standardize converter platforms across a build-out programme rather than engineer each one from scratch.

MPSS-BESS Offshore Storage Hub

An offshore battery hub that firms the farm — storage sited where the power is made.

Curtailment and grid congestion strand offshore-wind revenue. The MPSS-BESS houses LFP battery racks in the hull's pontoon and void space, with a full PCS, EMS and a fire-safety architecture built to DNV-RU-SHIP Pt.6 Ch.2 and the ABS Battery guide. It stores at the point of generation and dispatches into the export cable when the grid — or the price — is ready.

Why it wins

  • Marinized fire safety first. Thermal-runaway isolation between rack bays, gas detection, compartment segregation and restricted-access firefighting per class rules.
  • Hull-integrated racks use pontoon and void space — storage without a separate barge or reclaimed land.
  • Firms & arbitrages intermittent output; defers grid-reinforcement capex.
  • Same classed host as the converter and production variants — one supply chain, one training basis.
Built for

Offshore-wind developers and IPPs firming output, utilities and grid operators deferring onshore reinforcement, and merchant storage players chasing price arbitrage at the point of generation.

MPSS-O&G Production Topsides · the original use case

Deepwater production topsides on a dry-tree-capable, storage-ready host.

The mission the MPSS was born for. A full separation, compression and treatment train sits on the large open deck, with steel catenary risers on hull porches (LANG System pairing) and up to 2 M bbl of onboard storage in the SWIS configuration. The low-motion hull strengthens the SCR and dry-tree case and pulls first oil forward by taking the host off the critical path.

Why it wins

  • Host off the critical path. Leaseable, pre-engineered hull compresses FID-to-first-oil.
  • SCR & dry-tree capable. Ample deck and stability margin; riser porches on the hull.
  • FPSO-class storage optional — up to 2 M bbl (SWIS) removes the need for a separate tanker where there's no pipeline offtake.
  • Room to grow. Skid-on modules for added tiebacks, compression, produced-water or brownfield debottlenecking.
Built for

Deepwater E&P operators and their EPC contractors who need a predictable production host — and the option to redeploy it, or convert it to power, CCS or hydrogen duty in second life.
