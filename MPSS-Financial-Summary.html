At a glance

The offering in one screen

MILC owns MPSS hulls and leases them. The lessee (a grid operator, energy major, or developer) brings the topsides and runs the business; MILC provides the floating asset on a long-term, dry (bareboat-style) charter. Because an MPSS hull costs a fraction of comparable offshore tonnage, it can be leased below market day-rates and still generate a far superior return on capital.

02
The wedge

Why the return is structural, not optimistic

Every return figure above is driven by one fact: the hull is 10–40× cheaper than the tonnage it competes against. Because hull cost is the denominator of every yield calculation, the advantage holds across any realistic day-rate — it lives in the CAPEX, not the assumptions.

Vessel classHull costDay-rateRevenue yield on hull capital*
Ultra-deepwater drillship (7th/8th-gen)~$875M~$425k~16%/yr
Harsh-environment semisub (Norway CAT-D class)~$440M~$448k~33%/yr
MPSS-derived leased hull (mid case)~$45M~$350k~270%/yr

Pricing headroom. To earn the same return on hull capital as a drillship, an MPSS would need to charge only ~$22k/day — roughly a tenth of its target rate. The gap between that and the $200–500k the market pays for large semisubmersible tonnage is pure capital-cost advantage, available either as margin or as a competitive price cut.

*Revenue yield on hull capital = day-rate × 365 × utilization ÷ hull cost. A capital-efficiency ratio (not a profit margin); it excludes operating cost, financing, and topsides. Comparable hull costs and day-rates are sourced 2025–26 class averages.

04
The capital

Structure, use of funds & returns

Use of funds — first unit (~$50M)

  • ~$45M — hull fabrication (Hanwha Ocean), financed against the anchor lease.
  • ~$5M — development: FEED, class re-engagement (LR/ABS/DNV), fabrication-cost confirmation, business development.

Financing approach

  • Hull: offshore project / asset-finance debt against the signed charter (FPSO template), typically 60–80% of cost; ECA support via the Korean yard lowers the coupon.
  • Development tranche: founder / strategic equity or grant.
  • Balance & growth: strategic and infrastructure equity.

Why the returns compound

  • Fast unlevered payback (3–8 months of EBITDA against hull cost) means the asset self-funds quickly; with 60–80% debt, the equity cheque is small and equity payback faster still. Levered equity IRRs are modelled at term-sheet stage against actual debt terms.
  • Real residual value. A 50–70-year hull amortized over ~20 years is substantially deleveraged by the end of its first 10–15-year lease, then re-let 3–5 times. Terminal value is a class-maintained hull with 35+ years of fatigue life — not scrap.
  • Serial economics. Each additional unit prices toward the $25–35M serial target, widening the yield further as the fleet scales.

06
Integrity of the numbers

Assumptions, caveats & key risks

Assumptions & caveats

  • Figures are EBITDA-level — exclude financing cost, tax and depreciation; payback is unlevered cash.
  • Day-rate is a target band, not a signed rate; the first LOI converts it to contracted revenue.
  • Hull cost is an estimate pending Hanwha Ocean confirmation; serial target $25–35M.
  • 95% availability assumed (industry-typical 85–95%).
  • Comparable hull costs/day-rates are sourced class averages, not single named fixtures.

Key risks & mitigants

  • Fabrication cost unconfirmed → Hanwha engagement is the gating action.
  • No anchor lessee yet → no speculative build; traction campaign underway (grid / energy majors).
  • Class approval is historic (1989) → full LR/ABS/DNV re-engagement budgeted.
  • First-of-class discount → accepted on unit 1 if term ≥ 10 yr; recovered on units 2–5.
