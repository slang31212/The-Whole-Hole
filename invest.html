The Thesis in One Minute

Everything a patient family balance sheet is built to own.

Family offices don't chase the market — they buy durable things that pay. The MPSS is exactly that shape of asset: tangible, income-producing, defensively contracted, and long-lived. Four reasons it belongs on a private balance sheet.

⚓

Hard-asset collateral

Your capital sits behind 10,000 Lt of mild-steel hull classed by Lloyd's Register, ABS and DNV — not a covenant, not a brand, not goodwill. If a charter ends, the asset is still there, still classed, and still leaseable to the next field.

📈

Contracted, uncorrelated income

Revenue comes from multi-year day-rate charters to offshore operators — cash flow driven by field economics and steel availability, not by equity multiples or rate cycles. A genuine diversifier next to public stocks, credit and real estate.

🛡️

Downside built in by design

Tested to a 37.5 m survival wave with broken-mooring survival. Single operating draft, deep-draft low motion, huge stability margin. The engineering that keeps it producing in a storm is the same engineering that protects the residual value your capital is secured against.

♻️

A second and third life

When one field winds down, the same hull floats off and re-leases — added tiebacks, power, compression, CCS, hydrogen, data centres, accommodation. Optionality that keeps the asset earning far beyond a single charter and supports a real exit value.

Cost-to-Value

Why a $500k/day rate is a discount, not a premium.

Offshore day-rates are set by the value of the work, not by what the host cost to build. Production hosts of comparable capacity are multi-billion-dollar, multi-year bespoke builds. The MPSS delivers that class of host from a standardised hull built with commodity shipyard steel — and gets an operator to first oil years earlier. That spread is the asset.

The thesis does not depend on a low build cost. The hull is built from commodity shipyard steel, so it is a small figure by construction — but even at three times any indicative target it would still be little more than one percent of a host like Argos or Scarborough. Whether the yard delivers it for a lot or a little, the day-rate and the operator's saving barely move. The cost is not the argument; the value delivered is.

At $500k/day, the MPSS still removes a decade of host risk — and pulls first oil forward 5–7 years:

  • First oil 5–7 years early
  • A classed, known, de-risked hull
  • No bespoke host build programme
  • Billions in host capital avoided or deferred
  • Confidence it will work — on day one
The day-rate reflects the value delivered — a decade of risk removed and years of production pulled forward — not the cost to build. The spread between a ~$45M standardised hull and a billion-dollar bespoke host is the moat this asset owns.

How You Can Come In

Structures that suit a private balance sheet.

There is no single template. We shape the position around your mandate — income-first, growth-first, or capital-secured — across four common structures.

Income-first

Preferred equity

A fixed preferred return with priority over sponsor equity, secured against the asset. The closest thing to a bond coupon, backed by classed steel rather than a credit rating.

Balanced

Co-investment SPV

Own a direct, pro-rata slice of a single host through a dedicated special-purpose vehicle. Current cash yield from the charter plus your share of the residual value at exit.

Capital-secured

Sale-leaseback

Acquire the hull and lease it straight back to the operating company on a long charter. You hold the real asset; the operator holds the use. Clean separation of ownership and operation.

Growth

Platform equity

Back the standardisation of the fleet itself — several hulls, staggered charters, a diversified book of leaseable offshore infrastructure with re-deployment optionality across fields.

Independent Validation

Modelled by an independent petroleum economist.

Aberdeen University Petroleum & Economic Consultants (AUPEC), directed by Prof. Alexander G. Kemp — specialist adviser to the UK House of Commons Select Committee on Energy — independently studied the MPSS across the UK, Norwegian, Danish and Dutch North Sea sectors. Their finding was unambiguous: the MPSS is “anticipated to cost a fraction of a conventional purpose-built semi-submersible,” and across every modelled field it lifted post-tax returns to investors — turning marginal fields into attractive investments.
