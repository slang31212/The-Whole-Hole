Foundation

The core thesis & executive summary

The one thing to internalize

We are not selling a data center, a wind platform, or an oil rig. We are selling a standardized, class-approved, low-motion floating deck — leased by the day — whose unrivaled advantage is capital cost.

An MPSS hull costs ~$25–45M all-in (serial target $25–35M). The large semisubmersible and drillship hulls it competes against cost $425M–$1.2B — 10× to 40× more. Because hull cost is the denominator of every return calculation, MPSS can charge a day-rate far below the market and still earn a dramatically higher yield on hull capital than any comparable vessel. The edge is in the CAPEX, not the day-rate.

The business (MILC). Own standardized MPSS hulls and dry-lease them to operators on 10–15 year firm terms at a target of $200,000–$500,000/day, delivered 30–36 months from signature. MILC's obligations are narrow — hull integrity, class maintenance, insurance structure — not operations. That contracted-cash-flow structure is exactly what offshore lenders already underwrite: the FPSO financing template.

Why it works, in one breath. A ~$45M hull earning $200–500k/day pays back in 3–8 months (pre-financing) and throws off $68–173M/yr in unit EBITDA. A 50–70-year hull is re-let 3–5 times over its life. Five units at the midpoint rate ≈ $602.6M/yr fleet EBITDA.

The asset

The asset & its credibility

The MPSS is a four-column, ring-pontoon semi-submersible built from standardized flat-panel box-girder blocks — no delicate bracing — using high-automation welding at ~18 man-hours/tonne vs. 150–180 for conventional semis. Reference specs (1988 Seaways data; Lloyd's pre-certification file 00/OA/89001; Glasgow University tests):

ParameterValue
Main deck area8,100 m²
Deck payload10,000 t (design 12,500 t; up to 30,000 t studied)
Displacement~53,000 t
Operating draft27.5 m constant; ~6 m deballasted for port
MotionHeave ~24 s, pitch/roll ~42 s; heave ±2.5 m in 15 m seas
Hull fatigue life50–70 years (Faulkner)
Storage variant (SWIS)up to 1,750,000 bbl in-hull

The 35-year independent-validation spine

This kills the "can it really work?" objection — the #1 barrier to a novel offshore lease.

  • Lloyd's Register — Department of Safety Technology risk analysis (Dr David S. Aldwinckle, Dept. Head): "there are no inherent weaknesses in the MPSS design concepts." Lloyd's "fully accept all of the design principles" and confirmed it "can be approved for the intended service"; main scantlings approved.
  • University of Glasgow — 9-month model tank-test programme (Professors Faulkner & Incecik).
  • W.S. Atkins Oil & Gas Ltd. — independent structural/hydrodynamic analysis. ABS and DNV pre-qualification/review on record.
  • 1995 IMT / Enterprise Oil study — independent commercial validation; 12 shipyards priced it; "technically feasible and commercially viable."

Finance & leasing were always native to this design. Seaways' historical services included "Finance and MPSS leasing"; in 1989 Lloyds Bank Canada (Tim Plumtre) was ready to finance MPSS builds in Brazil and Newfoundland. We are re-activating a proven, bankable model — not inventing one.

Disclosure control (protect the moat)

General Arrangement behind NDA only; full technical package only under a formal engineering partnership or licence; GVA-lineage yards are the highest disclosure risk; lease documentation transfers no design rights.

Positioning & messaging

Positioning statement

For grid operators, energy majors, and offshore developers who need a large, stable, class-approved floating deck but cannot justify — or wait for — a $400M–$1B+ purpose-built hull, MILC leases the MPSS: a standardized, independently-validated, low-motion semi-submersible delivered in 30–36 months at $200–500k/day, with no construction risk, no residual risk, and redeployability at term end — because our hull costs a fraction of the alternatives and we finance it against your lease, not our balance sheet.

"Lease a class-approved, low-motion 8,100 m² offshore deck with 10,000 t payload on a 10–15 year term at $200,000–$500,000/day, delivered 30–36 months from signature. No construction risk, no residual risk, redeployable at term end."

Message pillars

1Capital efficiency you can't get anywhere else. 10–40× lower hull cost → underprice the market and still beat every comparable's return.
2De-risked and proven. Lloyd's, ABS, DNV, W.S. Atkins, Glasgow — 35 years of independent validation. Not a concept.
3Fast and standardized. 30–36 months to delivery; serial fabrication; a demand-aggregated production run, not a one-off.
4You bring the business, we bring the deck. Dry lease, narrow lessor obligations — you keep control of topsides, operations, and margin.

Two flagship angles that generate demand on their own

AThe substation / export gap → grid operators (TenneT) & energy majors (RWE). Floating wind farms routinely exclude the offshore substation, export cable, and grid-tie from headline floater CAPEX — a ~$1.1B gap on a 1 GW farm (~$17.0M per 15 MW position) developers must fund separately. The MPSS deck exists to close a gap they already have to pay for. This is the Cooper / ORE-Catapult "floating HVDC / power-island" track.

BThe cable-failure crisis → floating-wind operators, funders & insurers. Cable failures are 83% of offshore-wind insurance claims and a projected €61.5B over 2024–2035 (~3,600 failures; ~€29M each — the cost is the downtime, not the cable). Root cause is platform motion. MPSS's low-motion geometry (heave ~24 s, pitch/roll ~42 s — out of the 8–16 s swell-resonance band) cuts cable fatigue an estimated 40–50%: €2.8–5.8M per vessel over 25 years, a fleet NPV of €420–900M by Year 25. Operators buy cable performance, not complexity. Proof path: a 1–2 vessel instrumented pilot in the Celtic or North Sea.

Message-to-buyer mapping

  • Grid operator / developer (economic buyer): $/day vs. buying a hull; the substation gap they must fund anyway; delivery date vs. their COD.
  • Asset finance / CFO: dry-lease = clean, financeable cash flow; anchor-lease-financed, off balance sheet; payback & yield math.
  • Insurer / risk / lender: cable-fatigue reduction, class approval, hull fatigue life, low-motion data — lower loss ratios and premiums.
  • Sovereign / utility: buildable in-country (local content, jobs, tech transfer); resilience, desalination, defence.

Commercial

The offer & commercial model

Phase 1 — Bare-hull dry lease

MILC owns the hull; lessee fits and operates topsides. Obligations limited to hull integrity, class, insurance structure. $200–500k/day, 10–15 yr firm. This is the whole near-term business.

Phase 2 — Mooring package (Years 3–6)

Hull + installed station-keeping leased as one asset. Priced as an adder at the same lessor yield on mooring capex (~$10–20M site-dependent → roughly $4,000–8,000/day adder; firm only after site-specific design). Entry route: partner with a mooring contractor on the first two packages, internalize from unit three.

Phase 3 — SCR-integrated production host (Years 5+)

The coupled low-motion-host + Steel Catenary Riser architecture — the LANG System's original invention scope. Rates built line-item against field economics; reference is FPSO leasing ($540–780k/day on $0.7–1.3B capex). Begins once the leasing base generates recurring cash.

Commercial principles

  • Anchor lease first, then finance, then steel. No speculative construction — the signed lease is the key.
  • Sell yield, not sticker. Sit below market day-rates and still win on return. A first-of-class discount is acceptable on unit one if term ≥ 10 yr; recover on units 2–5.
  • Two rate cards: an internal cost basis (never external) and a two-page lessee-facing card in market-value framing only.
  • Redeployability & residual are part of the pitch — a class-maintained hull with 35+ years of remaining fatigue life at first-term end is real terminal value, not scrap.

Capital

Raising funds

The financing thesis

The anchor lease is signed first, and the hull is financed against the lease — the lender underwrites the charter, not the concept.

A dry-lease income stream with narrow lessor obligations is precisely what offshore lenders already know how to underwrite. Marketing/BD's job is to manufacture that anchor lease; the capital follows the contract. The marketing plan and the fundraising plan are the same plan.

What we're funding — and how small the first ask really is

  • First unit: ~$45M hull + ~$5M development ≈ $50M — trivially small against the $68–173M/yr one unit produces and the $425M–$1.2B cost of every competing hull.
  • Development capital (now, pre-lease): ~$5M for FEED, class re-engagement, fabrication confirmation, and the traction campaign → founder/strategic equity or grant.
  • Hull capex (post-anchor-lease): financed against the signed lease → offshore project / asset-finance debt.
  • Fleet growth: each 50–70-yr hull, re-let 3–5×, deleverages after ~20-yr amortization — strong recurring collateral to scale the fleet.

Fundraising funnel — run it like the named-account sale

  • Ongoing: replicate per hull; each lease expands borrowing capacity — the flywheel.
