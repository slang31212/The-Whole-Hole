About Us

Four decades in pursuit of a simpler way to work in the ocean.

Seaways exists to change how the offshore industry builds the platform at the heart of a deepwater development. For more than forty years we have pursued a single conviction: that the floating production host should be a standardized, validated product — not a bespoke, one-off project re-engineered from scratch for every field.

That idea began in 1980 with our founder, Craig Lang. Working as a structural engineer on deepwater tension-leg-platform mooring systems, he saw first-hand how much time, cost and risk the industry poured into custom-engineering each new host. He asked a simple question: why not build the floating host the way shipyards already build ships — in uniform, stiffened steel plate, with simple right-angle joints, on a repeatable panel line? The answer became the Multi-Purpose Semi-Submersible (MPSS).

The design that grew from that question is governed by five principles — Simple, Safe, Swift, Size and Saves. Together they describe a hull that is straightforward to build, inherently stable and survivable, quick to deliver, large enough to keep earning its keep, and economical across its whole life.

We have never asked the industry to take our word for it. The MPSS has been independently analyzed for hydrodynamic behavior, structural strength, fatigue, stability and mooring; tank-tested at model scale; and reviewed by the major classification societies — Lloyd's Register, ABS and DNV. Its engineering has been set out in the peer-reviewed literature, including the paper "Design Evaluation of a Multi-Purpose Semisubmersible", co-authored with Professor Douglas Faulkner of the University of Glasgow, and studied for doctoral research at Glasgow and Newcastle.

Today, Seaways offers the MPSS as leaseable offshore infrastructure — a proven host that operators can model, class, adapt and put to work before FID, compressing the time between a discovery and first production. Whatever the work, and whatever the ocean, our goal is the one Craig Lang set out with: to make working offshore simpler, safer and more economical.

The MPSS

Not new technology — proven technology, applied properly.

The Multi-Purpose Semi-Submersible uses standard ship-building methods: uniform box construction in stiffened mild-steel plate, assembled as pontoon, columns and a buoyant deck box with right-angle joints that are simple to weld and easy to inspect. It is less complex than other semi-submersibles, with excellent structural continuity — and it has been through extensive R&D, tank testing and independent classification review.

A standard hull, any mission

The same validated hull is adapted by its topside package — drilling, production, storage, accommodation or construction support. One design; many jobs.

Independently classed

Approved by Lloyd's Register, the American Bureau of Shipping and DNV — covering primary structure, intact & damaged stability, mooring and ballast systems.

Low-motion by design

A deep-draft ring pontoon sees roughly 40% of surface wave height at 27 m, giving very low heave response — effectively motionless in sub-12 m seas.

Applications

One platform. Many missions.

The MPSS is not a single-purpose floater — it's a scalable offshore infrastructure platform. The same validated 90 m × 90 m hull becomes power, data, water, food, wind or oil, defined only by the modules on its deck. One architecture; multiple offshore revenue models.

Up to ~300 MW of on-platform generation and ~100 MW of IT load, with seawater cooling and low PUE — for AI, HPC, cloud and colocation. Hydrogen-ready, low-carbon.

Deepwater production

The Design Philosophy

The Five S.

Every advantage of the MPSS traces back to five principles.

S

Simple

Modular, panel-line fabrication using common shipyard practice. Standard mild steel, no exotic procedures. Single operating draft with simple ballast and trim systems. Right-angle joints are accessible and easy to inspect.

S

Safe

A massive reserve of buoyancy — a "safety belt" of ballast at sea level. Watertight subdivision throughout and inherent flood-damage capability. Tested to a 37.5 m (123 ft) storm wave with no water on deck, no emergency draft, and broken-mooring survival.

S

Swift

A modular panel-line design means a hull delivered in about 14 months. Quality assurance is straightforward, and prefabricated topside modules skid on before deck assembly — enabling fast turnaround during retrofit.

S

Size

In the ocean, bigger is better. Bigger is more stable, holds more, stores more oil, and can be reconfigured. Ample deck and stability margin support steel catenary risers and dry trees.

S

Saves

Low initial cost, low maintenance and low operating cost through standardized box construction — plus high residual value. The sooner it's built, the sooner it makes money.

Ahead of its time

The figures once reported were the ones the market could accept — not the ones the MPSS could carry.

When it was first presented, the MPSS was such a departure from the state-of-the-art vessels of the day that its real numbers were hard to believe. To be taken seriously, payload and cost were quoted conservatively — a reported 10,000 t deck payload for a hull that ships more than 40,000 t of ballast water and carries far more. The design always did more than it was allowed to claim.

The industry has since caught up. The semi-submersibles that followed now quote comparable payloads — much of it counted in their own truss decks — at far higher cost and complexity. The MPSS got there first, with a simpler hull and a lower price. These pages state the design's real figures.

Hull Comparison

Same job. A fundamentally different hull.

Most deepwater floating production units (FPUs) are built on twin, ship-form pontoons with braced columns. The MPSS replaces that with a continuous ring pontoon and uniform box construction at a deep single draft — fewer parts, more deck, and lower motion.

The Business

Leaseable offshore infrastructure — not a one-off build.

For deepwater, the host hull is effectively long-lead equipment. The MPSS converts it from a custom project item into available leased infrastructure. A premium lease rate can be paid for by accelerated revenue alone — if first oil is pulled forward even by months.

Flexible commercial structures

Second-life & residual value

The MPSS keeps accepting work after first production — added tiebacks, extra modules, brownfield debottlenecking, power, compression, CCS, hydrogen, or float-off / float-on redeployment to a new field. That optionality supports high residual value.
