Lease vs. Build

Why a ready host sells — even when the majors build their own.

The model below prices the asset. This is the case for it. A standard host that already exists — or has its design frozen, long-lead items ordered and a yard slot held — is not competing with an operator's in-house host programme. It sells them the one thing that programme can never be: fast. That is the whole trade.

Build your own host

  • A new host engineered around one field
  • 3–4 years of custom design before steel is cut
  • A yard slot queued 2–4 years out in a tight market
  • First-of-a-kind cost and schedule risk on the buyer
  • Billions committed at FID before host risk is settled
  • Revenue delayed by host delivery
→

Lease a ready MPSS

  • A validated, class-approved host — LR, ABS & DNV
  • Design known, motions known, fabrication known
  • ~14-month hull delivery from a held slot
  • First-of-a-kind risk already retired
  • Capex shifts to opex — a lease, not a balance-sheet build
  • First oil pulled forward by a year or more
"The majors already do this" cuts for the MPSS, not against it. Every bespoke host is slow, first-of-a-kind and capital-locked for years. A ready host is the option they can exercise the moment a discovery, an outgrown tieback or a redevelopment appears.

The case, in four parts

You're selling schedule

Deepwater value is won or lost between host selection and first production. Pulling first oil forward a year or two on a field doing 60–100 kbbl/d is worth hundreds of millions in NPV. That delta is the price umbrella the lease sits under.

The addressable set is the long tail

Not just the flagship greenfield. It's the pile of marginal discoveries, near-field accumulations and outgrown subsea tiebacks that don't clear the hurdle rate against a $1–3 B bespoke host — but do against a leaseable one. Every major and NOC has a drawer full of them. That is the "other fields in the portfolio."

The leasing market already exists

FPSO leasing — Modec, SBM, Yinson, BW, Bumi Armada — is a multi-billion-dollar business built on exactly this logic: shift the floater off the balance sheet. The MPSS ports that model to the production host. It also tells you the earliest buyers are independents and NOCs, not the majors — capital-constrained, with no in-house host engineering.

A ready asset is a bidding war

When yards are full, steel and rates are high and two operators need a host in the same window, the one who can't wait pays a premium. Replacement cost plus time-to-replicate is scarcity value — the same dynamic that spikes rig and FPSO day-rates in every up-cycle.

What a sharp buyer will push on

Financing steel on spec

The honest weak point isn't demand — it's committing to a hull without an anchor charter. The answer is you don't build blind: freeze the design, hold the yard slot, order the long-lead items. That alone promises a delivery date years inside any bespoke competitor's — which is what triggers the bidding war — without carrying a finished hull looking for a home.

Field fit

Metocean (the 37.5 m survival wave), water depth, payload, storage and local-content rules vary by field. A spec unit has to be "good enough" across a target basin, not perfect for one field. Standardization is sized to a design envelope: pick the basin, then sell into everything inside it — topsides adapt, the hull doesn't.
